About CU Propulsion Laboratory

Established in Fall 2026 at the University of Colorado Boulder, CU Propulsion Laboratory (CUPL) is a student-led research and engineering organization of 30+ members dedicated to designing, manufacturing, testing, and operating real combustion and electrical propulsion systems.

Established

Fall 2026 at the University of Colorado Boulder.

Active Membership

30+ undergraduate and graduate students across aerospace, electrical, mechanical, and computer engineering disciplines.

Research Over Competition

Rather than rushing toward a single competition launch deadline, we prioritize empirical data collection, simulation validation, failure analysis, and open documentation.

Mission

To provide members rigorous and safe hands-on experiences in designing, manufacturing, testing, and operating the hardware and software of various propulsion systems.

Practical Engineering Execution

Apply classroom theories such as thermodynamics, fluid dynamics, and mechatronics to real-world machining, fabrication, and testing.

Interdisciplinary Collaboration

Build collaborative skills across small interdisciplinary teams and expand engineering perspective beyond a single major.

Safe Operation

Embed safety awareness and strict protocol adherence so members can actively identify and mitigate project hazards.

Continuous Knowledge Base

Carry documentation, test telemetry, and operational findings forward across semesters into a persistent technical archive.

What makes CUPL different

Most aerospace clubs specialize in a single flight vehicle or competition category. CUPL is built as a multi-architecture propulsion research lab.

Broad Propulsion Scope

We span both combustion propulsion (impulse jets, jet engines, turbofans, piston engines, afterburners, thrust vectoring, reaction control) and electrical propulsion (brushless motors, kinetic energy recovery, ionic thrusters).

Small, Accountable Teams

Projects run in focused, specialized sub-teams with clear ownership over CAD, simulation, fabrication, electronics, and testing—paired with upperclassman-to-underclassman mentorship.

Semester-by-Semester Cadence

Every semester concludes with a formal, publicly archived engineering report covering design methodology, test telemetry, simulation comparisons, bill of materials, and data analysis.

Reusable Technical Testbeds

Completed projects remain foundational baseline models and testbeds for future cohorts—and CU Boulder senior capstone teams (such as ASEN 4018/4028)—to build upon.

Campus facilities & tools

All manufacturing and hazardous testing are conducted strictly within certified CU Boulder spaces under written Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) and EHS guidelines.

Workshops & Test Cells

Fabrication and static testing utilize the Idea Forge, ITLL Manufacturing Center, Aerospace Machine Shop, and the Aerospace Building Test Cell.

Design & Simulation Stack

Teams model and validate hardware using Onshape, Fusion 360, Ansys Fluent & CFX, Siemens STAR-CCM+, MATLAB, and Arduino-based ECU programming.
